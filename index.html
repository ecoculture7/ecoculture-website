Why we exist

Three problems. One crisis.

Tired soil, chemical residue in everyday food, and farmers in debt look like three separate problems. They are one loop, and the same loop runs through a Delhi kitchen and a Bundi field.

The soil is being farmed to exhaustion

Decades of chemical fertiliser have stripped farmland of its organic matter. Each year the land needs more to give the same.

You check the vegetables. Nobody checks the atta.

Residue in rice, wheat and dal is silent. Most food sold as "natural" cannot say which field it came from, so nobody can check.

The farmer pays for all of it

Input costs rise every season; the mandi price does not. A small farmer borrows to sow and sells to repay, and the arithmetic never turns in their favour.

The choice we made

Natural farming, not organic.

Organic is a sound system, built for farms that can carry its cost. It swaps chemical inputs for certified organic ones — but the farmer still buys them from the market, every season, and pays for the certificate that says so. For a small or marginal farmer that is the same dependency with a different label.

Natural farming — प्राकृतिक खेती, Prakritik Kheti — makes the inputs on the farm, from what the farm already has. The cost of entry is close to nothing, which is why it is the only system a one-acre farmer can stay in.

We hold no organic certification and claim none: our food is naturally grown. We are working toward PGS certification, a participatory system verified by farmers' peers and designed for exactly these farms.

Ecology · Economy · Wellness

Everyone has to win, or the model doesn't work.

Every decision at Ecoculture is passed through three questions. If the answer to any one of them is no, we don't do it.

Ecology

पारिस्थितिकी

Does it restore the land? Soil life, water and the insects that pollinate — every field in the network is a step back from decades of chemical damage.

Economy

अर्थव्यवस्था

Does it make the farmer prosperous? No input bill, no middleman, a price fixed before sowing and paid at the scale. A farmer who prospers is a farmer who stays natural.

Wellness

स्वास्थ्य

Does it serve your health? Food tested for residue, traced to a field, with the report in your hands — not a logo on the pack.

I grew up in Uttar Pradesh watching chemical farming hollow out the soil and the farmer both. What struck me wasn't only the land. Membership

You're not subscribing to a box. You're joining a harvest.

There is no membership fee. You pay for your food in advance, that money buys the harvest at the weigh-in, and the discount is what we pay you for the use of it. The longer you prepay, the more it is worth — which is why the discount is not flat.
