Why we started

Nobody checks the atta. So we started checking.

Rice, atta, dal and oil are sixty to seventy per cent of what a family eats, and they are the one part of the kitchen nobody thinks to question. People wash the vegetables and trust the sack.

The farmers who grow that staple food are often caught in a different trap: spending more on inputs, selling at a price someone else sets. We think the two problems have one answer, and that answer is food you can trace to a person.

From the founder.

I was born in Uttar Pradesh, surrounded by farmland that had been fed chemicals for decades. By the time I was old enough to understand what I was seeing, the damage was visible: compacted soil, tired yields, farmers borrowing a little more each season just to break even.

What struck me was not only the soil. It was the trap. I did not start Ecoculture because it looked like a good business. I started it because I could not see another way to change what I was watching.

I trained in natural farming at NCONF Ghaziabad and worked with FreshFarms.com, where I saw the other half of the problem: people in cities who wanted clean food and had no way to know whether they were getting it. Ecoculture is the link between those two groups.
