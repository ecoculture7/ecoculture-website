Prices

Two prices on every pack. Here is how the higher one is set.

Every pack carries an MRP and a selling price. You pay the selling price. The MRP is not a number we invented to strike through: it is what the certified-organic shelf charged for the closest equivalent item, read on a dated run of each brand's own website.

The rule

For each pack we look up the closest like-for-like product at certified-organic brands sold online in India. We read the price on the brand's own site, never a marketplace listing. Where more than one of them sells it, we take the highest. That figure is the MRP. We compare against the category, the variety and the processing, and we do not name the brands.

Where there is no like-for-like item, or the shelf price is at or below our own, the MRP falls back to our selling price plus 5%, rounded up to the rupee. Three items fall there today, and they show only a token gap. We would rather show a small, true gap than invent a comparator.

Why this and not a formula

An MRP built from our costs would publish our cost stack and produce gaps from 15% to 38% with no logic you could see. A shelf-anchored MRP is a true statement about the market, means the same thing on every pack, and tells you nothing about our margins.

How it moves

  • The MRP is fixed for each label print run and read again at the next one, not more than once a quarter.
  • The selling price moves freely beneath it and never exceeds it.
  • Both figures include GST.
  • No brand is named on a pack, on a product page, or here. The MRP says what the certified-organic shelf charged for the closest equivalent item on a dated run; it is a statement about the category, not about any one seller.
