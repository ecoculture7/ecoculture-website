The lab report

A certificate says a system was followed. A lab report says what's in your bag.

The organic staples most people already doubt are certified. The logo is on the pack, and it still hasn't settled the question. So we'd rather hand you the actual test.

Because we buy from two districts, the report can name the field its lot came from. A lab report from an anonymous supply chain is a claim. One attached to a named plot is evidence.

What you receive

One report per lot. It travels with your order.

Each report carries a lot code, the result, where and when it was tested, what was screened for, and then the trail back: who grew it, in which village, when it was harvested, when it was milled and what the farmer was paid at the weigh-in.

The report is the claim. Everything below it is how you check that it is about your food and not somebody else's.

Start to finish

How a lot is verified.

1 · At the farm

The weigh-in

The crop is weighed and recorded against the farmer's name and field, and the farmer is paid at that weigh-in. The lot gets its code there.

2 · Sealed

The lot stays one lot

Cleaned and graded by the producer company's self-help groups, then sealed. It is recorded against the members whose commitment bought it.

3 · Sampled

A sample goes to the lab

A sample from the sealed lot goes to a NABL-accredited laboratory for a residue panel. A retained sample stays with us.

4 · Packed

Milled, packed, shipped

Atta is milled in NCR the week it ships; spices are ground the same week. The report for that lot goes out with your order.

Naturally grown. Not organic.

We hold no certification and claim none. Until PGS certification is in hand we say naturally grown. The word "organic" appears in our registered company name and nowhere else.

What a clean report does not prove

It shows that the screened residues were not detected in that sample, on that day. It does not describe the farm's every practice. That is why we also name the farmer, so you can ask.
